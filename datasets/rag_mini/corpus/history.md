# History of the Lumen Valley Water Cooperative

The Lumen Valley Water Cooperative (LVWC) is a member-owned utility that supplies drinking water to households, farms and small businesses across the Lumen Valley. This document summarises how the cooperative came to be and the milestones that shaped it.

## Founding

The cooperative was founded on 12 April 1958 by a group of 47 farming families who were tired of relying on shallow private wells that ran dry every August. The founding meeting took place in the back room of the Orrin Creek Grange Hall, and the first chair of the cooperative was a dairy farmer named Agnes Thornbury. Each founding family contributed 25 dollars toward the cost of drilling a shared deep well.

The first deep well, known today as Well No. 1, was completed in the autumn of 1959 and reached a depth of 210 metres. It served the original members through a single 9-kilometre pipeline along Valley Road.

## Growth and expansion

Through the 1960s and 1970s the cooperative grew steadily as new families moved into the valley. In 1974 the cooperative built the Harrow Hill Reservoir, a covered storage tank holding 3.2 million litres, which allowed it to keep pressure steady during summer peaks.

The most significant expansion came in 1991, when LVWC merged with the smaller Copper Ford Water Association. The merger added the town of Copper Ford and roughly 600 connections to the system. After the merger, the cooperative adopted its current name; before 1991 it had been called the Orrin Creek Mutual Water Company.

## Modern era

In 2004 the cooperative opened the Sedge Point Treatment Plant, which uses sand filtration and ultraviolet disinfection. The plant can treat up to 14 million litres per day and replaced the simple chlorination station that had been in use since 1962.

In 2016 the cooperative completed a valley-wide smart meter rollout, replacing every mechanical meter with a radio-read unit. The project took 22 months and cost 4.1 million dollars, funded partly by a state infrastructure grant.

## The cooperative today

Today the cooperative serves approximately 9,400 active connections and maintains about 380 kilometres of water mains. It employs 52 full-time staff, and its headquarters is located at 220 Millrace Lane in the town of Brindle. The cooperative celebrated its 65th anniversary in April 2023 with an open house at the Sedge Point plant that drew more than 1,100 visitors.

Although it has grown far beyond its farming roots, LVWC remains a not-for-profit cooperative: any surplus revenue is reinvested in infrastructure or returned to members as patronage credits.
