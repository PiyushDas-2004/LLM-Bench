# Water Quality Testing

Lumen Valley Water Cooperative tests its water far more often than the minimum required by regulation. This document describes the sampling program, the laboratory that analyses the samples, and how results are shared with members.

## Sources of supply

The cooperative draws water from two sources: four deep groundwater wells in the northern valley and a surface intake on the Sedge River. Roughly 70 percent of the annual supply comes from the wells and 30 percent from the river. All water passes through the Sedge Point Treatment Plant before entering the distribution system.

## Sampling program

Water quality technicians collect samples at 36 designated sampling stations spread across the distribution system. Bacteriological samples, which check for total coliform and E. coli, are collected at 12 of these stations every week, rotating so that every station is sampled at least once each month.

Other parameters follow their own schedules:

- Chlorine residual is measured continuously at the treatment plant and manually at every sampling station during each visit.
- Nitrate is tested monthly at each well, because the wells lie beneath agricultural land.
- Lead and copper are tested every three years at 40 homes chosen from a list of older properties.
- A full panel of more than 90 regulated chemicals is run once a year in October.

## Laboratory

All samples are analysed by Fennwick Analytical Laboratory, an independent laboratory located in the city of Harlow Bend that holds state certification for drinking water analysis. Samples must reach the laboratory within 24 hours of collection, so technicians deliver them by courier each afternoon.

## Action levels

The cooperative's internal action level for nitrate is 7 milligrams per litre, which is lower than the regulatory limit of 10 milligrams per litre. If a well exceeds the internal action level in two consecutive monthly tests, it is taken out of service and its water is blended or replaced with river water until levels fall.

## Reporting results

Every year by 1 July, the cooperative publishes its Annual Water Quality Report, which summarises all test results from the previous calendar year. A printed copy is mailed to every member, and the report is also posted on the member portal. Weekly bacteriological results are posted online within five business days.

## Member testing requests

Members who are concerned about their water can request a free in-home test for chlorine, pH and hardness once per calendar year. Requests are made through Member Services, and a technician usually visits within 10 business days. Testing for lead at the tap is offered for a fee of 35 dollars.
