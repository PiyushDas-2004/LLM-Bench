# Outage and Emergency Procedures

Water service interruptions can be planned, for maintenance and upgrades, or unplanned, caused by main breaks, power failures or contamination events. This document explains how Lumen Valley Water Cooperative handles each kind of outage and what members should expect.

## Reporting a problem

Members who notice a leak, a sudden loss of pressure, or discoloured water should call the 24-hour emergency line at 555-0100. Reports can also be made through the member portal, but the portal is monitored only during business hours, so urgent problems should always be phoned in.

## Planned outages

For planned work, the cooperative notifies every affected member at least 72 hours in advance by text message, email or door hanger. Planned outages are scheduled between 9:00 and 15:00 on weekdays whenever possible and are normally limited to a maximum of 6 hours. If planned work runs longer than 6 hours, the crew supervisor must arrange a temporary water tanker for the affected street.

## Unplanned outages

When a main breaks, the on-call crew aims to arrive on site within 60 minutes of the first report. The cooperative's target is to restore service to 90 percent of affected members within 8 hours. Status updates are posted on the outage map in the member portal every 2 hours until service is restored.

During a large outage, bottled water distribution points are opened at the Brindle Community Centre and at the Copper Ford Fire Hall.

## Boil-water notices

If pressure in a section of the system drops below 140 kilopascals, there is a risk that contaminants could enter the pipes. In that case the cooperative issues a precautionary boil-water notice for the affected area. Members under a boil-water notice should bring tap water to a rolling boil for at least one minute before drinking it or using it for cooking.

A boil-water notice is lifted only after two consecutive sets of bacteriological samples, taken at least 24 hours apart, both show no coliform bacteria.

## Bill credits

Members whose service is interrupted by an unplanned outage lasting more than 24 consecutive hours receive an automatic bill credit of 10 dollars for each full day without service. Planned outages do not qualify for credits.

## Preparedness

The cooperative recommends that each household keep an emergency supply of at least 4 litres of drinking water per person per day, for three days. Backup generators at the Sedge Point Treatment Plant and at all three pumping stations can keep the system running for up to 72 hours during a regional power failure.
